## §1. The goals

**As the owner wrote them (verbatim, 2026-09-30):**

> 1. LLM story coherence and improv: That the LLM is able to go along and
>    improvise in the story without repeating itself and forgetting key parts
>    of the interaction
> 2. TTS with emotions in support of the narration: that the TTS audio
>    synthesis works well with real voices, and it is able to convey credible
>    emotions, in sync with the story.
> 3. Automated deployment to Cloud GPU: that the whole AI component of the
>    project can be easily installed in a cloud VM that provides a Ubuntu OS,
>    a capable CUDA GPU, and is able to run Docker containers with access to
>    the CUDA device. (this we have already demonstrated)
> 4. Automated deployment to Local GPU: that the whole AI component of the
>    project can be easily installed in a local PC running Ubuntu, with a RTX
>    3090 GPU (or better)

**As published** (the READMEs' wording; the owner asked for mistakes to be
corrected, and there were none of substance — the edits are light):

1. **Story coherence and improvisation.** The LLM improvises the story as it
   goes, without repeating itself and without forgetting key parts of the
   interaction — including what a listener told the cast.
2. **Emotional voices in support of the narration.** Speech synthesis with
   real human voices conveys credible emotions, in sync with the story.
3. **Automated deployment to a cloud GPU.** The whole AI side of the project
   installs easily on a cloud VM with Ubuntu, a capable CUDA GPU, and Docker
   with access to that GPU. *(Already demonstrated.)*
4. **Automated deployment to a local GPU.** The same installation works on a
   local PC running Ubuntu with an RTX 3090 (24 GB) or better.

The character bibles are not among them — the owner: "for the demo that is a
minor detail".

## §3. Each goal — where it stands, what is missing

The agent's reading of the records, 2026-09-30.

1. **Story coherence and improvisation — largely met.** The owner's listen of
   2026-09-28 (the fork's run `2026-09-28T13-43-28`): "Wow, big improvement
   in story coherence… All in all I am satisfied with where we are."; 0 of
   215 model lines near-repeated (step 3.4c.8, the repeat guard, dropped).
   Improvisation is the feature (the owner's ruling of 2026-09-28, 13:14).
   **What serves "without forgetting":** the trim fires every 22-36 rounds
   (measured 2026-09-28) and drops the middle of the script — the contacts
   with earlier callers among it. Three follow-ups address it — the trim's
   thresholds in settings, a context budget near the full 16k, a 32k
   context — and a show fix before the talk, names-only A (code tells the
   model which earlier caller a voice is; the follow-up "A listener memory
   keyed by identity").
2. **Emotional voices — the biggest gap.** Real voices are in: a first cast
   of EARS voices since 2026-09-29 ([discussion 2026-09-29]
   voice-datasets-with-emotion §11.5). But each character has **one**
   reference clip, the neutral one, and the app does not send a line's mood
   to the voice engine — emotion comes through only as far as the engine
   reads it from the words. **Credible emotions in sync with the story need
   the "Mood clips" feature** (the follow-up of that name: the page sends the
   mood, the TTS route picks `ref-<mood>.wav` with fallbacks; about 2-3 hours
   in the fork, the agent's estimate). The casting script already writes
   the mood clips (`--with-moods`), and the mood mapping has a first draft
   (§11.3 of that discussion). How much of a clip's emotion carries into the
   cloned voice is unmeasured.
3. **Cloud GPU — met.** Proven live on a fresh A6000 box on 2026-09-18 — the
   whole stack from zero, one command, a second run changing nothing
   (`changed=0`) — and many times since; a from-zero deploy takes about 7
   minutes (measured 2026-09-22). It relies on provider images that bring
   the NVIDIA driver and Docker (proven: "R570 CUDA 12.8 with Docker" and
   "Ubuntu Server 22.04 LTS R550 CUDA 12.4 with Docker"); the playbook
   checks that the driver can run the pinned CUDA version before installing
   anything.
4. **Local GPU — built, never run.** The playbook's local target
   (`delegate_to: localhost`) exists since 2026-09-17 but has not been run;
   the owner action queue's item 3, "Check the home 3090 box's NVIDIA driver",
   is still marked deprioritized. **What is missing:** that driver check,
   then a deployment on the 3090, proven as the cloud one was (from zero,
   then `changed=0`). The 3090's 24 GB fits the stack (the spec's 24 GB target
   is the RTX 3090 class). How the laptop's app reaches the local box — over
   the LAN or through the same kind of tunnel — is to be decided.

> **This is a pool of creative goals generated by the agent** (2026-09-30),
> not goals stated by the owner. The owner's four goals are §1; how the owner
> classified this pool by its use to the project is §6. The pool is saved
> exactly as the agent wrote it, at the owner's request: "Let's save it
> exactly as you wrote it".

Here are ten ideas, drawn from what the project already has and what it's
close to. After the list I say which four I'd add.

### The ideas

**1. The audience changes the story (the biggest gap in your list).** Someone
in the room holds the talk key and answers the radio. The cast answer them,
ask their name and where they are, and later greet them by name when they call
back.
- **Why it matters:** this is the product's own twist ("the radio sometimes
  listens", spec §1). Your list mentions "the interaction" only in passing.
- **What exists:** the listener's turn and Contact mode; the restatement of
  what earlier callers said. **Missing:** names-only A, already planned as a
  show fix before the talk (about 1.5-3 h).

**2. Behind the curtain: the director you can watch.** The audience sees the
machinery beside the show: which round the director chose and why, the
instruction the model got, the grammar keeping it in format, the lines
streaming in.
- **Why it matters:** it's the Python part of the story at a Python meetup: a
  director in plain Python, a grammar constraining an LLM, the browser as
  clock.
- **What exists:** the debug line under each round, and the debug files.
  **Missing:** a view designed to be shown on a projector.

**3. A scientist turns: a voice that changes live.** Mid-show, one character
is infected. Recasting their voice step by step (neutral → pain → distress)
makes the turning audible, and the story's events can announce the bite.
- **Why it matters:** it's pure theatre, and it shows off something we built
  yesterday. The app re-reads `ref.wav` on every line, so a recast during the
  show is heard on the next line.
- **What exists:** the casting script and the EARS emotions. **Missing:** a
  way to trigger it (a key, or a scheduled beat) plus the events. Small, and
  risky only in taste.

**4. The show never dies on stage.** A dropped tunnel, a failed request or a
slow box doesn't end the demo: Resume picks up, the dead-air static covers
pauses, and if all else fails the canned episode plays.
- **What exists:** Resume, and the canned episode is already a MUST (Task 7).
  **Missing:** stating it as a goal, and a rehearsal that pulls the plug on
  purpose.

**5. What it costs.** "An evening of AI radio theatre for about fifty cents
an hour": one rented A6000, open models, no API bills. A 7-minute deploy,
then pennies.
- **What exists:** the provider survey's prices and the measured deploy time.
  **Missing:** a verified current price and one slide. Almost free.

**6. Structure without a script: the same seed replays the same show.** Two
runs with the same seed give the same show word for word; a new seed gives a
new story. It shows an LLM kept on a leash that's reproducible, not random
chaos.
- **What exists:** the seed setting, and a replay proven on 2026-09-22.
  **Missing:** a short, rehearsed demonstration.

**7. The period stagecraft.** The show in the looks: the 1950 radio with its
magic eye, the ham transmitter with its oscilloscope.
- **What exists:** built (`tz-0.3`). **Missing:** stating it as a goal, maybe
  with the dead-air static.

**8. The audience writes the news.** People in the room send an event from
their phones ("a helicopter circles the lab"), and the director works it into
the broadcast, read out as a fixed line.
- **Why it matters:** the whole room plays, not only the person at the
  microphone.
- **What exists:** the director's events and fixed lines. **Missing:** a small
  web form and an endpoint to queue an event, plus care about what strangers
  type. About half a day; the moderation question is the hard part.

**9. A third deployment target: a Mac.** The whole stack on an Apple Silicon
Mac: llama.cpp on Metal, tts-serve's MLX engine.
- **What exists:** a follow-up, parked until after the MVP. The demo laptop
  (16 GB) can't do it; your 64 GB Mac might. **Missing:** everything, so this
  is a stretch after the deadline.

**10. Built with an AI pair, and a memory that survives.** A meta-goal: how
the project was built with an AI coding agent, with `docs/` as the memory of
record, pre-registered experiments and review before every commit. The "tight
learning loop".
- **What exists:** all of it, in the repository. **Missing:** only the
  decision to talk about it. That's your call on the talk's framing.

### My recommendation

**Add four:** **1 (the audience changes the story)**, **2 (behind the
curtain)**, **4 (the show never dies)** and **5 (what it costs)**.
- They fill real gaps in the list, and they suit a Python meetup.
- Three need little new work: 1 is already planned, 4 overlaps Task 7, 5 is a
  slide.
- **3 (a scientist turns)** is my favourite for pure theatre, but it's the
  most creative risk with eight days left. I'd hold it as a stretch goal.

## §7. Addendum, 2026-09-30 — idea 8, the audience writes the news: only through a third-party gateway, and deferred

**The owner (verbatim):** "I particularly like 8 (the audience writes the
news), however it is a challenge, remember that the TalkWithZombies app runs
only in the local machine (address loopback), so we would need to create a
whole new web app for this, and it won't work if the backend is a local
3090..., no, the only way would be to collect the ideas via email, or some
chat channel gateway (Telegram, Whatsapp, etc). I doubt we will have time to
execute. Do you want to push back against this statement?"

**The agent's pushback** — on two facts, not on the timing:

1. **The GPU backend does not matter.** The app, TalkWithZombies, always runs
   on the laptop; the GPU box — cloud or the 3090 — only serves the three
   models. An audience event would enter the app on the laptop, and the
   director would turn it into a fixed line there, whichever backend serves
   the models.
2. **It would not need a whole new web app.** The loopback binding is real —
   uvicorn runs on `127.0.0.1`, and the show page must be on `localhost` for
   the browser to allow the microphone — but phones need only a form, not the
   show page: the agent proposed a tiny second listener on the laptop, bound
   to its network address, serving one page with a text box that queues an
   event, while the show app stays on loopback (spec §8, single operator);
   about half a day. The real obstacles, as the agent saw them: the venue's
   Wi-Fi (public networks often isolate devices from each other; the
   fallbacks: the laptop's own hotspot — which costs the laptop the venue's
   Internet that the tunnel needs — or the owner's chat gateway) and what
   strangers type (read aloud in a cloned voice; it needs an approval queue
   the owner works on the laptop). On time the agent agreed: idea 8 fits only
   if the four goals, names-only A and the canned episode land early.

**The owner's ruling (verbatim):** "No, no, I do not want an open port in my
laptop to accept any incoming connection, in a venue with an infrastructure
I've never seen... That's a recipe for disaster.... Not to mention I would
have to make an exception in my local firewall. You do not think I leave any
connection reach any open port, right? Who do you think I am... A frontend
dev? 😛 No, I would only consider this if the public interaction happens via
a third party gateway, as I already explained."

**What stands:**

- **No inbound connection to the laptop, ever** — no open port, no firewall
  exception. The laptop only makes outbound connections, as it already does
  for the SSH tunnel to the model services. The agent withdrew the
  local-listener shape.
- **The only acceptable shape is a third-party gateway** — email, or a chat
  channel (Telegram, WhatsApp, and the like). A gateway fits the rule when it
  is read **from** the laptop: for example a Telegram bot polled over
  outbound HTTPS — nothing listening, nothing exposed. Still needed: the
  approval queue on the laptop (every submission is read aloud in a cloned
  voice), and the gateway's credential (a bot token) kept as a secret, out of
  both repositories.
- **Where the backend runs is irrelevant to idea 8** — the agent's first
  point, not contested.
- **Deferred.** Idea 8 is interesting (§6, group 3) but waits behind the
  owner's four goals (§1), names-only A and the canned episode (Task 7) — the
  owner: "I doubt we will have time to execute."

## §9. Addendum, 2026-10-07 — idea 3, a scientist turns: it needs a story line, not only a voice

With more days before the talk, the agent proposed idea 3 again, as the most theatrical of the talk's "What's next",
estimating it "low to medium" risk — "mostly the page and the director". The owner turned it down twice over.

**The voice (the owner, verbatim):** "I do not like "A scientist turns"... Where are we going to find the voice samples
for the TTS engine that show one of the EARS voices slowly changing into a more zombified state? ... We would need to
reach into some audio processing software and add distortions to the current voice samples... That is a nightmare of
voice processing and adds nothing to the technical side I want to demonstrate." (The agent's shape had applied the
effects in the browser as the voice plays, with no new samples; it agreed on the point that matters — it adds nothing
to the technical story the owner wants to tell. The only technically real version it knew: IndexTTS, one of
tts-serve's engines, blends a second "emotion" clip into the voice with a strength, `emotion_alpha`, which could rise
over the rounds — at the cost of switching voice engines.)

**The story (the owner, verbatim):** "When we were discussing the "one scientist turns" feature, I think you failed to
realize that this feature, besides requiring voice effects, requires also introducing a new "story line" (and
therefor would overlap with the feature of introducing story plots or quests to guide the narration), making a
scientist turn would necessitate narrative support, otherwise the LLM would have no idea that the voice is changing
and there would be no reaction of the cast to the voice changing... Therefore what you proposed required also opening
this much bigger feature (I don't remember if we called it story lines or story plots or quests?). Do you agree? If so
please document this observation where the task is recorded."

**The agent agrees.** §5's entry did name the bite as an event ("the story's events can announce the bite") and a
trigger as missing, but treated the narrative as one more event. It is not: the director draws each event once and
moves on (the follow-up "Events that stay on topic for a few rounds"), and the model knows only what the director
tells it each round. For the cast to react as one of them turns — the bite, the fever, the voice going wrong, the
others' fear and decisions — the director must carry that thread across many rounds: a state of its own ("Ralph is
turning, stage 2 of 4"), instructions that follow it, an ending. That is a **story line** — the bigger feature the
owner names, which in this project's records is closest to the follow-ups "Episodes — a story arc, with a recap
between episodes" and "Events that stay on topic for a few rounds"; no record calls it plots or quests. So idea 3
depends on that feature, and the agent's "low to medium" risk was wrong: it left out the larger half of the work.

**Status:** not executed; the talk's "What's next" slide keeps "A scientist turns" as an idea. If taken up, it comes
after a story-line mechanism, as its first use.
